## 1. Ameliorative Strategy

Bert identified an underweighting of ameliorative work — research that has direct, real-world impact — across his project portfolio. Five strategic moves were defined to correct this imbalance:

1. **Ship Good-Thinking-Bot** (highest single-project impact). A working educational tool deployed in a course is worth more than a half-dozen unfinished prototypes.
2. **Package teaching innovations as reusable tools.** Foil Bots, the MARC platform, and related work should be distributable artifacts, not one-off course features.
3. **Thermo econ paper for practitioners.** The framing/ordering work with Nicholas Potter targets a behavioral economics audience that acts on findings.
4. **Disagreement as diagnostic tool.** Move beyond the pure-math theorem toward applications: institutional design, policy deliberation, conflict mediation.
5. **Scale chai-t + plan-and-learn-modeling together.** IMCI's modeling core and the pedagogical modeling mode share infrastructure and philosophy; growing them in tandem multiplies impact.

**Meta-principle:** Treat deployment as research. The act of putting a tool in front of users generates empirical questions (what do students actually struggle with? where does the bot fail?) that feed back into the theoretical program. Deployment is not the end of the research pipeline — it is a stage within it.

**Goal:** Bring the entire 24,761-record corpus to 100% Pydantic validity.

### Starting Conditions

QC1 (March 23) had identified 126 schema issues and 91 track mismatches without fixing them. QC2 (March 23) had flagged 56 mechanical verification failures in decision theory and game theory records. Of those 56, 51 had been removed during earlier QC passes and 2 had already been corrected, leaving 1 unresolved.

### Issues Fixed

| Issue | Count |
|-------|-------|
| Invalid JSON lines removed | 18 |

| Rubric format fixes in `qc_fill_thin.jsonl` | 475 |

**B6.2-ROYAL-001** was a Prisoner's Dilemma scenario that falsely claimed two Nash equilibria. The correct analysis has exactly one Nash equilibrium (mutual defection). The record was corrected rather than removed, preserving the scenario while fixing the game-theoretic content.

**Type coercions** were overwhelmingly in `game_hooks.npc_dialogue_seed`, where the MindRouter synthetic pipeline (gpt-oss-120b) sometimes returned arrays of strings instead of a single string. Lists were joined with spaces; dicts were serialized.

### Result

## 3. Taxonomy Gap Analysis

A systematic comparison of Bert's PHIL 4040 (Rationality and Strategic Interactions) lecture notes for weeks 9 through 16 against the existing 167-code taxonomy.

### Method

Lecture notes covering decision theory, game theory, social choice theory, and behavioral game theory were read and every distinct concept was extracted. Each concept was matched against the taxonomy. Unmatched concepts were grouped by theme and assessed for pedagogical importance to the bot.

### Key Findings

- **40 concepts** identified in the lecture notes have no representation in the taxonomy
- **~81 problem sets** from PHIL 4040 are available for adaptation into corpus records
- **Pareto efficiency** (C1.3): mentioned 149 times as a secondary concept across existing records but had no dedicated taxonomy code — the single largest gap
- **Social choice theory** (Condorcet's Paradox, Arrow's Impossibility Theorem, voting systems): entirely absent despite being a natural bridge between game theory and political philosophy
- **Behavioral game theory** (level-k thinking, social preferences): partially covered through cognitive bias codes but not as formal game-theoretic concepts

**Report:** `reports/taxonomy-gap-analysis.md`

## 5. Benchmark Results

Four models were benchmarked against all 182 anchors (167 original plus 15 new) in three modes: identify, classify, and explain. The judge model was qwen3.5-400b.

### Current Benchmarks (182 anchors)

| Model | Parameters | Identify | Explain |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
| qwen2.5:7b | 7B | 2.52 | 2.22 |
| gpt-oss-20b | 20B | 2.61 | 2.41 |
| gpt-oss-120b | 120B | 3.15 | 2.93 |
| Nemotron-3-Super-120b | 120B | 3.40 | 3.54 |

All scores are on a 1-to-4 scale.

### Previous Benchmarks (167 anchors, for comparison)

| Model | Parameters | Identify | Explain |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
| gpt-oss-20b | 20B | 2.62 | 2.47 |
| gpt-oss-120b | 120B | 2.95 | 3.04 |
| qwen3.5-122b | 122B | 3.47 | 3.60 |

### Key Findings

1. **Nemotron-3-Super-120b is the new champion.** At 3.40/3.54 (identify/explain), it is comparable to qwen3.5-122b's earlier 3.47/3.60 and substantially outperforms gpt-oss-120b on the expanded anchor set.

2. **qwen2.5:7b has substantial headroom.** As the fine-tuning candidate, its 2.22 Explain score leaves room to push toward 3.0 or higher with QLoRA training on the 28K-record corpus.

3. **The new codes are harder.** The 15 PHIL 4040 codes (B6.4 through B15.3) pulled down averages slightly compared to the 167-anchor baselines. This is expected — social choice and behavioral game theory are conceptually demanding.

4. **Weakest areas across all models:** Dennett's thinking tools, game theory (especially the new codes), and argument structure basics. These represent the bot's distinctive pedagogical value and are the primary targets for fine-tuning.

### Track-Level Scores: qwen2.5:7b Explain Mode

These scores define the fine-tuning targets.

| Track | Score |
|-------|-------|
| Cognitive biases | 2.62 |
| Microeconomics | 2.50 |
| Decision theory | 2.26 |
| Logic and critical thinking | 2.22 |
| Game theory | 2.16 |
| Dennett's tools | 1.21 |

Dennett's tools at 1.21 is the single weakest track. This makes sense — Dennett's "intuition pumps" and "thinking tools" are idiosyncratic philosophical concepts that do not appear in standard training data. The corpus includes ~800 Dennett records specifically to address this gap.

## 7. Next Steps: QLoRA Fine-Tuning Plan

### Base Model

**qwen2.5:7b** — available on MindRouter, small enough for on-device inference, with a 2.52/2.22 baseline that leaves clear room for improvement.

### Method

QLoRA (quantized LoRA). Nick Potter's proof-of-concept implementation lives in `qlora_merge/` and supports five adapter merging strategies: linear, task arithmetic, TIES, DARE, and router-based.

### Training Pipeline

1. **Convert corpus to instruction-tuning format.** Each record's scenario becomes the user prompt; the expected response includes structured analysis (pattern identification, explanation, assessment).
2. **QLoRA training on qwen2.5:7b** using the University of Idaho HPC cluster (SLURM). The 28,038 records are the training set.
3. **Evaluate post-training** against the same 182-anchor benchmark with the same judge (qwen3.5-400b) for direct comparison.
4. **Target scores:** Push Explain from 2.22 toward 3.0 or higher overall, with specific targets of game theory from 2.16 to 2.8+ and Dennett's tools from 1.21 to 2.0+.

### Deployment Path

1. Upload the fine-tuned model and corpus to HuggingFace under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license
2. Deploy on HuggingFace Spaces (ZeroGPU) with a Gradio interface for browser-based interaction
3. Pilot in a course (likely PHIL 1103 Intro to Ethics or a critical thinking section)

### Alternative: Nemotron Distillation

An alternative to training directly on the corpus: use Nemotron-3-Super-120b (the new benchmark champion at 3.54 Explain) as a distillation teacher. Generate high-quality responses from Nemotron for each of the 28,038 training scenarios, then train qwen2.5:7b on those responses. This teacher-student approach could be more effective than training on the corpus records directly, since the teacher responses would model the kind of nuanced explanation the bot should produce.
